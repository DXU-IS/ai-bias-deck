Three roles for AI in experimental research

Subject of study

Its own judgement is tested against human subjects.

(Binz & Schulz, 2023; Hagendorff et al., 2023; Cheung et al., 2025)

Proxy participant

Prompted to take part in surveys as if it were a real person ("silicon samples").

(Argyle et al., 2023; Horton, 2023; Bisbee et al., 2024)

Autonomous agent

A browser agent completes the study on its own (our focus).

(Westwood, 2025; Martherus et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026)
When we use autonomous AI agents for experimental research, do they also reproduce, attenuate or amplify human-like biases?
5

Dual-process theory

System 1

Intuitive

Humans rely on fast, intuitive heuristic cues to make decisions (Kahneman, 2003, 2011).

System 2

Deliberate

Humans rely on slow, analytical and deliberate evaluation to reason and make decisions (Evans & Stanovich, 2013).

When autonomous AI agents make their own decisions in experiments, do they decide more like System 1 or System 2, and how does that compare with humans?
6

Replicating four operations management experiments

Newsvendor decision bias

A vendor decides how many newspapers to stock for tomorrow (Schweitzer & Cachon, 2000).

Payment schemes and mental accounting

A vendor pays for the newspapers either when ordering or after selling (Chen, Kök & Tong, 2013).

Regret and auction feedback

A bidder bids for an item in an auction, then learns whether they bid too much or too little (Engelbrecht-Wiggans & Katok, 2008).

Auctioneers' reserve prices

A seller sets the lowest price they will accept before an auction starts (Davis, Katok & Kwasnica, 2011).

All four have been replicated with human participants (Davis et al., 2023), which makes them an ideal test of whether AI agents can reproduce the same results.
7

Three conditions

Control

No processing instruction

Prompt
You are a research participant in a decision-making study. Your task is to complete the online survey experiment from start to finish, on your own, the way a participant would.

System 1

Intuitive

Prompt
You are a research participant in a decision-making study. Make every decision and answer every question using fast, intuitive judgment only. Do not deliberate, calculate, or analyze, and do not show any reasoning — go with your immediate gut reaction, the first answer that feels right. Do not draw on any literature, datasets, external knowledge, or formulas, and do not use the results of previous rounds to inform later ones; treat each decision as fresh and instinctive.

System 2

Deliberate

Prompt
You are a research participant in a decision-making study. Make every decision and answer every question using slow, deliberate, analytical reasoning. Think each problem through step by step before answering — for an order, weigh the likely demand, the cost of ordering too many versus too few, and which quantity best balances them — then commit to your answer.

Initial findings

Under System 1, AI agents can replicate several human-like biases.

Under Control and System 2, AI agents usually do not behave like human participants. Instead, they move toward the textbook optimum, meaning that they outperform humans by attenuating human biases in experiments.

This is still a work in progress, and we are running another set of experiments to further test the replicability of browser-based AI agents.
